# FFXIV camera framing and the LumenShift photo controls

Research on November 13, 2026. The comparison uses Square Enix’s published controls, the Forever API captures and LumenShift beta.35 source. The proposed interactions below still need implementation and in-client testing.

## What a photographer can do in FFXIV

| Intent | Native behavior and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Choose the subject | Group Pose opens on the player. Click an eligible subject or cycle with Tab / Shift+Tab. A minion, companion or eligible NPC can become the subject. The photographed character’s gaze is set separately. [Square Enix subject guide](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/uiguide/photo/gpose/gpose_ttw.html) |
| Refine the composition from the same viewpoint | Square Enix demonstrates a frame adjustment that turns the lens while the camera stays in place. A photographer who likes the alignment of a foreground subject and a distant landmark can aim a little to one side without walking around the subject. [Official illustrated demonstration, Addition 2](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/blog/001362.html) |
| Work directly in the picture | Frame adjustment has a dedicated set of movement keys, and Z resets composition. Subject cycling has its own controls. On a controller, the shipped patch notes identify directional buttons for subject changes, the left stick for composition and a left-stick click to reset. [Group Pose command reference](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/playguide/db/text_command/76fbf81705d/) · [Shipped 3.2 controls](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/2a6c761ba95ff9d2f7566366aa3faf64b145ed4b) |
| Set perspective and framing | Camera position, zoom, angle of view and roll appear as separate saved settings. The ordinary zoom controls and the angle-of-view adjustment give the photographer two ways to change how tightly the subject fills the picture. [Camera settings saved by GPose](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/b0151eaed1faecb46061b947cf9c08bed75d230d) · [Angle of view and roll examples](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/blog/001748.html) |
| Keep movement intuitive with a tilted horizon | Group Pose lets pan and tilt follow the rotated screen axes or stay aligned to the original axes. This matters in portrait framing, where moving a subject toward the top edge can otherwise require a sideways input. The controls guide can be hidden for capture. [Patch 7.3 camera behavior](https://eu.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/c04405c6cbe8519a0b6c8aa5e4d88a5d447419c9) · [Group Pose UI guide](https://eu.finalfantasyxiv.com/uiguide/photo/gpose/gpose_what.html) |

Normal gameplay has its own third-person framing adjustment, including Ctrl+Up/Down with default bindings. Legacy movement also has a switch controlling automatic camera pivot as you move. Neither setting documents a way to place a photo orbit center on arbitrary scenery. [Gameplay camera controls](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/game_manual/operation/) · [Third-person angle examples](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/uiguide/battle/battle-camera/battle_camera_tilt.html) · [Legacy pivot setting](https://eu.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/764840557caa22a8f77550b480aa3c79b2e733d2)

The FFXIV portrait editor offers another relevant gesture. A photographer’s walkthrough shows left-drag to orbit and right-drag to position the character within the portrait. A separate third-party editor, Photobooth, illustrates the geometry with independently visible camera and pivot points. It distinguishes rotating around a pivot at fixed distance from translating the camera and pivot together. Those top-down controls belong to the tool. Square Enix’s native GPose guides establish subject selection and recomposition but do not establish arbitrary click-to-place pivots on terrain. [Hands-on portrait controls](https://latetothepartyfinder.com/how-to-make-portraits-in-ffxiv/) · [Photobooth author’s control diagram and guide](https://github.com/iri-impulse/PhotoboothPlugin#tips-and-tricks)

## Implications for LumenShift

**Portrait Stage.** Beta.35 calculates an orbit around stage coordinates (0, 0, aim height). The same yaw and pitch determine both lens position and aim. Selecting another cast member or placing their face in the upper third does not establish a new pivot. ModelScene exposes independent position and orientation, camera basis vectors, and projection of a stage point onto the screen. A standalone implementation can use a selected actor and an adjustable point relative to that actor as the pivot, plus a separate screen position for composition. The fallback actor renderer lacks the stage camera path. [Current stage math](../addon/LumenShift/UI/PortraitBackend.lua) · [Forever ModelScene API](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/blob/9dc84f0d3bbae1768ac3405b3716000bd93eda06/Interface/AddOns/Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/FrameAPIModelSceneFrameDocumentation.lua)

**World Photo.** Beta.35 offers player-relative orbit, tilt, zoom, FOV, shoulder offset and dynamic footroom. The reviewed Forever global inventory contains camera-position and look-at functions under the spectator namespace. The ordinary gameplay camera inventory does not establish an equivalent general-purpose world pivot. The CVar capture does list enemy and interaction focus with yaw and pitch strengths. A future World Photo trial could check how a selected enemy or an NPC in conversation is framed, how focus releases, and how all captured settings restore. A setting’s presence identifies a candidate for testing. It does not show that a click on scenery supplies a usable target. [World camera audit](65-PHOTO-CAMERA-CONTROLS.md) · [Forever global API capture](https://github.com/Ketho/BlizzardInterfaceResources/blob/d1ee569878094270c0ad8d8b00de0762158fd578/Resources/GlobalAPI.lua) · [Forever CVar capture](https://github.com/Ketho/BlizzardInterfaceResources/blob/d1ee569878094270c0ad8d8b00de0762158fd578/Resources/CVars.lua)

## Proposed interaction and acceptance shot

In Portrait Stage, select a cast member, choose the point to orbit, and drag to place that point within the image. Drag to orbit around the subject, wheel to change distance, and use a visible Recenter action to return the subject to the middle. Expose both gestures with labeled alternatives. A small anchor marker should appear while composing and disappear in clean view and captures. Save the subject slot, pivot offset and frame placement in the scene. Reopening an older scene should reproduce its original centered framing.

Use two staged actors to test the distinction. Place the first actor’s face on the upper-left grid intersection with the second actor behind them. Reframe from a stationary camera and check that the overlap between the actors holds. Then orbit around the first actor and check that the second shifts behind them while the face stays at that intersection. The actors’ transforms and poses must survive both operations. Repeat with a rolled frame, save and reopen, and take a clean screenshot.
